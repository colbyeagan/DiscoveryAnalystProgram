Three Statement Model

A company reports its finances in three linked statements. Change a number in one, and watch exactly which lines move in the other two. Hover or tap the ? beside any line to learn what it is and where it connects.

How the statements fit together

Each statement answers a different question about the same company. They are built from the same transactions, so they have to agree.

Income statement

Did the company make a profit this year?

Revenue minus every cost, over a period of time. It ends with net income. Profit is an accounting measure and is not the same as cash.

Cash flow statement

Where did cash come from, and where did it go?

Starts from net income and adjusts it to real cash movements across operations, investing and financing. It ends with the cash balance.

Balance sheet

What does it own and owe right now?

A snapshot at one date. Everything the company has is paid for by borrowing money or by its owners.

Assets = Liabilities + Equity

  • Net incomeis the first line of the cash flow statement and, after dividends, increases retained earnings on the balance sheet.
  • Ending cashfrom the cash flow statement becomes the cash line on the balance sheet.
  • Changes in receivables, inventory, payables and the likeon the balance sheet become the working capital adjustments in operating cash flow.
  • Capital spending and depreciationmove property and equipment: capital spending adds to it, depreciation (an income statement expense) reduces it.
  • Borrowing, share issues and dividendsin financing cash flow change debt, common stock and retained earnings on the balance sheet.
